\section{Introduction}

Atom-to-atom mapping assigns corresponding atoms across a reaction. It is used to identify reaction centers, extract reaction templates, and prepare endpoint structures for mechanistic calculations. A reaction may support several chemically meaningful correspondences: different mappings can imply different reaction centers or bond-change patterns, while molecular symmetry can produce equivalent atom assignments. Retaining alternatives can therefore support analyses that would otherwise depend on an early choice of one mapping. The challenge is to distinguish structural alternatives from permutations that arise only from molecular symmetry.

Existing approaches use learned correspondence signals or graph-based optimization. RXNMapper derives mappings from attention in a reaction language model~\citep{schwaller2021}; LocalMapper learns from chemist-labeled reactions~\citep{chen2024}; and SLAPMapper combines iterative label refinement with sequential linear assignment~\citep{koda2025}. The Golden benchmark provides a common set of annotated reactions for assessing mapping recovery~\citep{lin2022}. For applications requiring alternatives, recovering the annotation somewhere in a returned family is a different objective from producing one correct top-ranked mapping.

Weighted endpoint graphs introduce a further distinction. A permissive matching tolerance can preserve a fragment despite changes in its bond orders, but the corresponding atom shuffles need not preserve the bond events assigned at a finer threshold. Replacing a compressed family by one representative can therefore hide distinct transformations. Likewise, greedily growing one fragment can prevent a neighboring fragment from reaching an alternative assignment.

MAPPA addresses these issues by branching over structural fragment choices while keeping symmetry-related atom bijections compressed (\Cref{fig:algorithm}). Growth maintains one compatible assignment; the complete compressed placements are constructed only when the fragment saturates. Default MAPPA uses one seed ordering per cut and the cut sweep. We introduce this sweep to vary the search conditions and counteract greedy fragment commitment, and also apply it to SLAP to test its benefit beyond fragment search. The final representation groups unordered matched fragment pairs while retaining their allowed mapping relations. Separate decoding collects unique fragments, computes their local bond-event responses, and composes those responses under the saved joint constraints. The method does not require the reference transformation to minimize the number of bond changes.

\section{Method}


Algorithm~\ref{alg:mappa-overview} connects the fragment-level operations in \Cref{fig:algorithm}. A live branch stores the fragments already placed, their atom assignments, and the compressed choices still allowed by those decisions. Growing and placing the next fragment conditions every later continuation. The matching routine is treated as an operation here; its compatibility tests and symmetry representation are defined below. For bidirectional search, the search phase is applied in each orientation, retaining the orientation of each returned family.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{MAPPA: fragment growth, compressed placements and separate decoding}
\label{alg:mappa-overview}
\small
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE Oriented endpoint graphs, seed orders, optional anchors, search budgets
\ENSURE Compressed final branches; optionally, local bond-event responses
\STATE $\mathcal S\gets\varnothing$ \COMMENT{collected terminal branches}
\FORALL{conditions: uncut source graph and each single-edge source mask}
  \FORALL{requested seed orders under this condition}
    \STATE Initialize live branches with the supplied anchors
    \REPEAT
      \FORALL{seeds in the current order}
        \FORALL{live branches eligible to grow from this seed}
          \STATE Grow a fragment using one-witness existence queries \COMMENT{Fig.~\ref{fig:algorithm}a}
          \IF{no admissible fragment placement is found}
            \STATE Retain this branch for later seeds
          \ELSE
            \STATE At saturation, construct each distinct compressed placement once
            \STATE Fork one continuation per retained placement
            \STATE Commit each placement; retain its compressed symmetry and constraints
            \STATE Enforce the cap at this hierarchy level and across live parents
          \ENDIF
        \ENDFOR
      \ENDFOR
    \UNTIL{all branches are finished or a full pass makes no progress}
    \STATE Add terminal branches and their search-stop status to $\mathcal S$
  \ENDFOR
\ENDFOR
\STATE Group final branches by unordered fragment pairs; reduce repeated family records
\STATE Expose compressed branches with constraints and provenance
\IF{bond-event decoding is requested as separate post-processing}
  \STATE Collect all unique fragments and their family and boundary references
  \STATE Compute shared local responses using the original matrices
  \STATE Compose requested joint choices under the saved constraints \COMMENT{Fig.~\ref{fig:algorithm}c}
\ENDIF
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

The forks in the search phase represent different fragment placements, not one branch for each symmetry-related bijection. The cut sweep changes the conditions of this search; a single mask can expose a mapping with several bond events. Event decoding is a separate operation on the retained output. Search caps are recorded as truncation, not treated as proof that no alternatives exist.

\subsection{Weighted matching and continuous growth}

Let $V_R,V_P$ be the reactant and product atom sets, $Z_R,Z_P$ their element labels, and $W_R,W_P$ their symmetric bond-weight matrices. A partial mapping $m:D\subseteq V_R\rightarrow V_P$ is injective and element compatible. A mapping assigning every source atom is bijective when the endpoint element counts agree. Explicit hydrogen atoms participate in the search. Molecular coordinates can accompany the graphs but do not determine the growth decisions.

The active source graph contains edges with weight at least $b$. Starting from a seed atom, MAPPA grows a connected source fragment $F$ and retains one compatible assignment as a witness. It chooses a boundary atom uniformly, then chooses one of its incident frontier edges uniformly; bond weight does not rank proposals. Let $A$ contain the preservation edges accepted so far. A proposal adds its selected edge to $A$ and asks whether an injective, element-compatible mapping exists satisfying
\begin{equation}
 Z_R(u)=Z_P(m(u)),\quad
 W_P(m(u),m(r))\geq b,\quad
 |W_R(u,r)-W_P(m(u),m(r))|\leq\tau_{\mathrm{iso}}
 \label{eq:match}
\end{equation}
for each $(u,r)\in A$. Only an accepted growth edge adds a preservation constraint. Other source bonds between included atoms are not implicitly required, and source nonedges do not impose negative constraints. Injectivity includes atoms assigned by established fragments.

The current witness is extended when possible. Otherwise, the existence query can change earlier assignments within the growing fragment while keeping completed-fragment assignments fixed. The same exact search either finds one witness or proves that none exists; compatibility, degree and injectivity checks eliminate impossible choices. Finding a witness accepts the edge and replaces the stored assignment. Proven absence defers the edge while other frontier edges are explored. A resource limit is not evidence of absence. Joining an established fragment preserves its locks and previously accepted constraints.

Growth continues while admissible frontier proposals remain. At saturation, completion uses all accepted constraints to construct the distinct compressed placements directly. It does not replay earlier candidate growth or enumerate symmetric bijections for later merging. Each retained placement then starts a continuation for the remaining atoms. A completed-fragment cache reuses results only when the endpoints, policies, locks, occupancy and relevant boundaries agree; eviction changes computation, not the allowed mappings.

This is a greedy search along each trajectory: an accepted extension can remove placements that would support a different fragment boundary. MAPPA broadens coverage through cut sweeps and seed orderings.

\subsection{Cut sweep to vary greedy fragment boundaries}
\label{sec:cut-sweep}

Our cut-sweep strategy reruns growth under alternative source-edge conditions. In addition to the uncut graph $G_R=(V_R,E_R)$, it searches a graph with one eligible edge $e$ removed, $G_R^{(e)}=(V_R,E_R\setminus\{e\})$, for each edge at or above the cut threshold. The evaluated cut threshold equals the active-graph floor $b=0.2$, and eligible edges include bonds to H. Each subrun retains the atom set and the original endpoint weight matrices.

Removing $e$ makes that frontier proposal unavailable, so its preservation test cannot be added through growth. An extension accepted in the uncut run may otherwise merge atoms into a fragment and exclude alternative placements. Under the cut condition, a fragment can stop at a different boundary, grow through another route, or leave atoms for a later seed. Omitting the constraint can also admit placements previously excluded by $e$. Thus the sweep counteracts greedy commitment by changing the conditions under which growth proceeds. It does not force the endpoints of $e$ into different final fragments or require a bond-breaking event: they may reconnect through other source edges, and the final correspondence may preserve their bond.

MAPPA takes the union of the uncut and single-edge subruns before final branch deduplication. The default one-seed mode uses one seed ordering per subrun, so it already includes the full cut sweep. There are $|E_R|+1$ subruns at the evaluated thresholds. A single-edge cut describes the search perturbation, not the number of bond events in a returned mapping. One removed constraint can redirect subsequent fragment growth and placements throughout the reaction, exposing an alternative with several bond formations, cleavages, or bond-order changes. MAPPA therefore need not enumerate combinations of final bond events to discover multi-event alternatives. Bond-event decoding uses the original matrices, so a search cut itself contributes no event or event-count penalty. Growth remains greedy within each conditioned subrun; the union broadens coverage without making the search exhaustive.

\begin{samepage}
\subsection{Automorphisms and correlated mapping families}

The distinction between structural branches and explicit bijections is central to the design. Even one fragment family can contain combinatorially many assignments: $k$ independently exchangeable methyl-hydrogen triplets admit $(3!)^k$ bijections. MAPPA stores interchangeable target pools and permitted correlated actions rather than listing those bijections. The branch cap applies at every hierarchy level, including the combined live continuations across parents. It counts compressed choices, not the assignments represented inside a pool, and does not guarantee a small total search.
\end{samepage}

Completion stores a representative assignment, interchangeable target pools, fixed roles and correlated permutation actions. A pool is valid only when every represented injective assignment satisfies the completed constraints. Distinct pool allocations are grouped only when an exact automorphism of the globally colored target graph relates them under the locks and matching context. Such an action can move atoms outside the fragment together with atoms inside it; orbit labels alone do not justify independent swaps. Automorphism computation uses nauty~\citep{mckay2014}.

Matching tolerance and automorphism equivalence have different roles. The relation $|x-y|\leq\tau_{\mathrm{iso}}$ is generally not transitive, so it does not by itself define a permutation group. Each accepted source edge $e$ instead defines an exact Boolean test on a target pair $(p,q)$,
\begin{equation}
 \chi_e(p,q)=\mathbf 1\!\left[W_P(p,q)\geq b\ \land\
 |W_P(p,q)-W_R(e)|\leq\tau_{\mathrm{iso}}\right].
 \label{eq:response}
\end{equation}
Interchangeability requires the same eligibility and support for these tests, together with the matching context. Completion depends on the finished constraints, rather than on how temporary witnesses changed during growth. Local pools and global correlated actions serve different purposes; neither can remove a required constraint.

The saved mapping relation also contains the constraints imposed by its search decisions. We represent a flat family $\mathcal F_j$ by a seed mapping $m_j$, a sequence of allowed action groups $G_{j,1},\ldots,G_{j,k}$, and final preservation constraints $C_j$:
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal F_j=\left\{a_k\circ\cdots\circ a_1\circ m_j:
 a_i\in G_{j,i},\ C_j(a_k\circ\cdots\circ a_1\circ m_j)\right\}.
 \label{eq:family}
\end{equation}
These actions encode correlated moves; atoms in a common orbit are not independently permutable. Disjoint actions commute and can be placed in a canonical order. The order of overlapping actions is retained. A sequence of groups is not generally equivalent to the group generated by their union.

The action sequence includes the allowed pool choices and correlated moves, conditioned on prior locks. Different constraints or action programs can define different families even when their representative atom sets agree. MAPPA retains these distinctions; sharing a fragment description does not enlarge its saved mapping relation.

\subsection{Unordered final branches}

During search, history and boundary conditions matter because they determine valid continuation. At completion, the branch identity is instead the unordered collection of matched atom sets,
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal B=\{(R_1,P_1),\ldots,(R_q,P_q)\},
 \qquad P_i=m(R_i),
 \label{eq:branch}
\end{equation}
under a fixed matching policy. Sorting atoms within each set and then sorting the fragment pairs removes dependence on growth order and fragment labels. Growing $A$ before $B$ or $B$ before $A$ yields one branch when the final paired sets agree. The colors within each candidate in \Cref{fig:alternatives} illustrate these matched fragment pairs.

Each branch stores the union of its flat families. Identical normalized family records are shared, while distinct constraints or action programs remain available. This transformation preserves the represented mapping union: it changes its grouping, not its admissible assignments. Provenance links retain the discovering paths, but the original search hierarchy is unnecessary for decoding. This conservative deduplication does not assert that the remaining families are mutually disjoint.

\subsection{Decoding from unique fragments}

For an unordered source pair $\{u,v\}$ whose atoms are both mapped, define the signed change by
\begin{equation}
 \Delta_{uv}(m)=W_P(m(u),m(v))-W_R(u,v),\qquad
 s_{uv}(m)=
 \begin{cases}
 +1,&\Delta_{uv}(m)>\delta_{uv},\\
 -1,&\Delta_{uv}(m)<-\delta_{uv},\\
 0,&\text{otherwise}.
 \end{cases}
 \label{eq:events}
\end{equation}
Positive events denote bond formation or strengthening; negative events denote cleavage or weakening. A change exactly equal to the threshold is not an event. The thresholds are $0.3$ for pairs involving a metal and $0.5$ otherwise, independently of $\tau_{\mathrm{iso}}$. Hydrogen atoms participate. For partial mappings, bonds crossing from mapped to unmatched atoms contribute losses on the source side and gains on the target side, without inventing an assignment for the unmatched atoms. Bonds wholly inside unmatched material are omitted. The event count $E(m)$ sums the nonzero responses, counting each bond once. All responses use the original endpoint matrices, including bonds omitted during the cut sweep.

Decoding starts after family reduction across all supplied seeds and cuts for one reaction and direction. It first collects the complete inventory of unique saved fragments, their occurrences in families, and their constraints, actions and boundary references. It then computes shared responses for internal bonds, bonds between fragments, and mapped/unmatched boundaries. Two occurrences share a response only when the relevant fragment, witness, ordered actions and boundary context agree. A cut is a search condition, not an independent decoding unit.

For a requested joint choice, the decoder validates the saved family constraints and combines the corresponding local responses. A shared symmetry action remains one correlated choice across every affected fragment. Alternative fragment partitions describe the same family and are not added together. This separates reusable local calculations from branch composition without rematching or expanding complete bijections. Matching-preserving shuffles can still yield different events, as in \Cref{fig:algorithm}c.

Ordinary decoding retains this local representation; it does not automatically enumerate distinct whole-reaction event classes or select a minimum-event mapping. Those are separate queries on the retained families. The archived coordinate evaluation used an explicit event-window query: event-invariant families were certified directly, and remaining choices were encoded in Z3~\citep{demoura2008} to obtain one witness per signed event class. Exhaustion or exclusion of every retained family certified completeness within $E\leq K$; timeouts remained unresolved. That certificate concerns saved search output within a window, not exhaustive structural search or the completeness of ordinary local decoding.

\subsection{Alternative correspondences and chemical interpretation}

An atom mapping summarizes the net correspondence resulting from a chemical process without specifying its intermediates or elementary steps. When that pathway is unknown, endpoint structures alone may not determine a unique correspondence. MAPPA retains mapping families and their correlated choices so that their bond events and conditional atom assignments can be queried. An explicit event-class query can supply representative witnesses and supporting families. Distinct patterns can have equal event counts, and minimizing the count need not recover the reference correspondence.

\Cref{fig:alternatives} illustrates both points with saved Golden case 9 witnesses. The annotated correspondence places the alcohol oxygen in the product ring and leaves the ketone oxygen in water (A). Another candidate exchanges these oxygen origins (B); a third also exchanges two carbon assignments (C). Candidate B has fewer total events than the reference, while A and C have equal totals but different correspondences. Thus, selecting either one bijection or only the lowest event count discards information about the alternatives. These are selected witnesses from the archived search, not an exhaustive decoded set or validated reaction pathways.

\section{Evaluation}

\subsection{Datasets and comparison protocol}

We report a completed validation of the one-witness/direct-completion pipeline on all 1,851 Golden reactions~\citep{lin2022}, alongside archived September studies of seed count, branch caps, comparator performance and 140 coordinate/WBO endpoint pairs. The archived studies used their recorded implementation and are not reruns of the current method. Their measurements and witnesses are retained with that scope.

Golden inputs use formal bond orders and explicit H, with reference labels removed before search. Recovery requires the annotated heavy-atom correspondence, including unmatched atoms, modulo endpoint chemical symmetries preserving charge, isotope, H count, and stereochemistry. These chemical colors define reference equivalence; they do not imply that element/WBO matching itself enforces every chemical attribute. Failures and unresolved cases remain in the denominator. This measures reference inclusion among returned alternatives, not their ranking.

LocalMapper provides the prior accuracy-SOTA baseline~\citep{chen2024}. Its published Golden accuracy was 89.8\% on 1,758 retained reactions with manual calibration. SLAP reported 86.9\% on that dataset version, counting reference inclusion among its candidates~\citep{koda2025}. These literature scores are separate from our strict re-evaluation. We additionally apply the cut sweep introduced here to SLAP; ``SLAP sweep'' denotes this extension, not a published SLAP score. It is our strongest evaluated prior-method baseline for alternative recovery. Released RXNMapper, Chython, Indigo, and RDT provide further comparisons~\citep{schwaller2021,chython,indigo,rahman2016}.

The coordinate collection contains 18--137 explicit atoms per endpoint, including organometallic and multicomponent structures. It was inspected during development and has no verified atom mappings: it is not a mapping-accuracy benchmark. The archived study compared the lowest event count found and the patterns achieving that count. MAPPA used supplied WBO matrices; SLAP used XYZ-derived binary graphs. Returned SLAP heavy-atom assignments received hydrogen refinement and were scored on the same WBO matrices as MAPPA. The comparison covers those saved witnesses, not all possible SLAP hydrogen assignments or a fresh current-implementation evaluation.

\section{Discussion}

\subsection{Applications and future directions}

Atom correspondence is a preprocessing decision in reaction-path interpolation: the assignment determines which product position each reactant atom approaches. Atomic-index assignment is a recognized obstacle in reaction-pathway search~\citep{de2019}. MAPPA's complete correspondences and allowed joint shuffles could be used to reorder and align endpoints before constructing initial paths for nudged elastic band (NEB) calculations~\citep{henkelman2000}. Different event patterns provide alternative reaction cores, while symmetry-related assignments within one pattern can give different interpolations. This offers a systematic way to prepare several TS-search starting points. Mapping the same core into a TS guess also allows its vibrational modes to be assessed against the intended bond changes.

Structure verification is another application. A generative model may return a molecule with reordered atoms or a different conformation, making direct coordinate comparison unsuitable for checking its connectivity. AAM supplies an explicit correspondence under which the target and generated structures can be compared for missing or extra connections. A complete witness that passes this comparison certifies agreement under the chosen graph representation and tolerances. Connectivity alone does not establish stereochemical identity or energetic stability, and failure to find a witness in a bounded search is not proof of a mismatch.

Such checks could also supply a verification mechanism for large language model (LLM) post-training, including reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR)~\citep{lambert2024}. For tasks that ask a model to generate a target structure or satisfy specified structural constraints, an independently checked atom correspondence could provide a task-specific reward. The reward would certify the stated structural conditions, with unresolved searches kept distinct from verified failures. This is a proposed use of the verifier; we have not evaluated an RLVR training loop or its effect on molecular generation.

For pathway and retrosynthesis analysis, alternative event patterns expose different atom origins, reaction centers, and possible fragment disconnections. These could help organize mechanistic hypotheses or compare proposed precursors while retaining symmetry-related choices for subsequent geometric analysis. One net correspondence can admit several intermediate sequences, however, and a compatible mapping need not describe a feasible reaction. Energies, reaction conditions, stereochemistry, and experimental evidence remain necessary to assess the proposed routes. The present evaluation concerns correspondence coverage and bond-event alternatives; it does not measure improvements in NEB convergence, TS-search success, or synthesis planning.

Alternative enumeration is not unique to MAPPA or SLAP; for example, an Ising-computing approach enumerates optimal mappings and reduces them by molecular symmetry~\citep{ali2025}. MAPPA's contribution is to retain conditional fragment families and expose their distinct event patterns and allowed shuffles without expanding all bijections.

\subsection{Coverage and remaining search limitations}

An audit of all eleven ten-order Golden misses from the archived study separates unmatched-atom decisions from fragment-placement failures (\Cref{tab:golden-misses}). It is not an audit of the separate current one-seed validation. In four archived reactions, every retained family maps exactly one more heavy-atom pair than the reference. Such a family cannot reproduce the reference's unmatched-atom pattern through symmetry shuffling: permutations preserve the number of assigned pairs. The remaining seven lack the required correspondence within their saved families. Re-ranking or expanding those families into explicit bijections cannot supply a missing structural alternative.

Two archived traces show how greedy extension excluded a representable reference. One reference exchanges adjacent labels in an otherwise unchanged pyridine ring; another exchanges the carbonyl-oxygen attachments of oxalic acid across its retained carbons. Growth preserved bonds that these correspondences require separating. Uncapped trajectories still missed both references, while reference-constrained fragment choices established their representability. In the oxalic-acid example, a reference-selected three-edge relaxation also exposed the reference to matching; the single-edge sweep did not contain that cut set. These are feasibility diagnostics, not additional benchmark recoveries or traces of the current matcher. For the other five correspondence misses, the audit did not isolate cap truncation from unsampled growth choices. They motivate selective alternatives to successful growth extensions and explicit choices about leaving atoms unmatched, rather than treating larger caps or more symmetry expansion as sufficient remedies.

Only one of the eleven misses is recovered by any tested comparator configuration, including the SLAP sweep. The shared failures warrant inspection of the supplied correspondences as well as search improvements; they do not establish annotation errors. All eleven remain in the reported denominator. Independent evaluation on annotated coordinate reactions is needed to assess generalization beyond the development collection.
