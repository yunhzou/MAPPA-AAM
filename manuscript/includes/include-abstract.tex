Atom-to-atom mapping traces atom identities from reactants to products, supporting reaction analysis and pathway construction. Yet the same endpoints can admit multiple mappings, and molecular symmetry makes explicit exploration of these alternatives expensive. We introduce MAPPA, a constraint-based framework that efficiently discovers and organizes alternative atom mappings. MAPPA combines greedy growth of shared structural fragments with a systematic bond-cut sweep to expose correspondences that individual growth trajectories can miss. Symmetry-related assignments are represented compactly, and a separate decoder reuses fragment calculations to organize alternatives under an approximate bond-event model. This separation supports parallel exploration and preserves alternatives for subsequent chemistry-aware selection. MAPPA achieves reference inclusion among decoded alternatives for 99.46\% of Golden reactions and 99.63\% of EnzymeMap reactions. In a separate paired Golden comparison, MAPPA achieves higher reference coverage while requiring approximately one-tenth of the search CPU time of an expanded SLAP cut-sweep baseline. Together, these results demonstrate broad reference recovery with efficient exploration and a reusable representation of mapping alternatives.
