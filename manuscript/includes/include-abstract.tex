Atom-to-atom mapping traces atom identities from reactants to products, supporting reaction analysis and pathway construction. Yet the same endpoints can admit multiple correspondences, and selecting a single answer can conceal alternatives with different chemical implications. We introduce MAPPA, a framework that efficiently discovers and retains alternative atom mappings. It uses shared molecular fragments and molecular symmetry to represent these alternatives compactly, allowing their bond changes to be examined and supporting subsequent chemistry-aware selection. MAPPA achieves the highest reference coverage among the evaluated methods on Golden, reaching 99.46\%, and recovers 99.63\% of EnzymeMap reference mappings. In the standard benchmark runs, mapping and decoding together average 3.19 CPU seconds per Golden reaction and 13.16 CPU seconds per EnzymeMap reaction. These results make broad exploration of mapping alternatives practical, providing a foundation for studying reaction ambiguity and incorporating chemical knowledge into mapping selection.
