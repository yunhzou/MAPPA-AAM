Atom-to-atom mapping traces atom identities from reactants to products, supporting reaction analysis and pathway construction. Yet the same endpoints can admit multiple mappings, and molecular symmetry makes explicit exploration of these alternatives expensive. We introduce MAPPA, a constraint-based framework that efficiently discovers and organizes alternative atom mappings. MAPPA combines greedy growth of shared structural fragments with a systematic bond-cut sweep to expose correspondences that individual growth trajectories can miss. Symmetry-related assignments are represented compactly, and a separate decoder reuses fragment calculations to organize alternatives under an approximate bond-event model. This separation supports parallel exploration and preserves alternatives for subsequent chemistry-aware selection. MAPPA achieves the highest reference coverage among the evaluated methods on Golden, with decoded reference recovery reaching 99.46\% on Golden and 99.63\% on EnzymeMap. With one seed, mapping and decoding together average 3.19 CPU seconds per Golden reaction and 13.16 CPU seconds per EnzymeMap reaction.
