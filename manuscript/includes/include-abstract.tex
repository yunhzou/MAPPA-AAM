Atom mapping is often treated as if every reaction had a single correspondence between reactant and product atoms. The same endpoints can support several plausible mappings, while molecular symmetry produces many equivalent atom assignments. We introduce MAPPA, a graph-based method that preserves alternative fragment placements without explicitly listing symmetry-related bijections. Fragment growth asks only whether one compatible assignment exists; at saturation, a separate operation constructs the distinct compressed placements under the completed constraints. A cut sweep varies the growth conditions, and a separate decoder shares local bond-event responses across unique saved fragments while preserving joint symmetry constraints. A completed Golden validation recovers the annotated correspondence among the retained alternatives for \CurrentGoldenRecovered{} of \CurrentGoldenTotal{} reactions (\CurrentGoldenPercent\%), with all verification outcomes resolved. Archived seed, cap and comparator studies provide additional evidence about coverage and search cost. An archived 140-reaction coordinate/Wiberg-bond-order study illustrates bond-event alternatives under unverified mappings; it is not a mapping-accuracy benchmark. MAPPA provides an inspectable representation of atom correspondence and bond changes for subsequent chemical assessment.
