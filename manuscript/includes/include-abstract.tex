Atom-to-atom mapping traces atom identities between molecular endpoints, supporting reaction analysis and pathway construction. Yet the same endpoints can admit multiple correspondences, and selecting a single answer can conceal alternatives with different chemical implications. We introduce MAPPA, a framework that efficiently discovers and retains alternative atom mappings. It uses shared molecular fragments and molecular symmetry to represent these alternatives compactly, allowing their bond changes to be examined and supporting subsequent chemistry-aware selection. MAPPA achieves the highest reference coverage among the evaluated methods on Golden. Standard benchmark runs recover 99.08\% of Golden and 99.63\% of EnzymeMap reference mappings, with matching and decoding together averaging 3.19 and 13.16 CPU seconds per reaction, respectively. Broader exploration raises Golden coverage to 99.57\%. These results make broad exploration of mapping alternatives practical, providing a foundation for studying reaction ambiguity and incorporating chemical knowledge into mapping selection.
