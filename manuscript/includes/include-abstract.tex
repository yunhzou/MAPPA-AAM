Atom mapping assigns corresponding atoms across a chemical reaction, but endpoint structures can support several assignments and symmetry can multiply their explicit representations. We introduce MAPPA, an alternative-generating graph-based method that separates correspondence discovery, atom-set reduction and bond-event decoding. Fragment growth asks only whether one compatible assignment exists. At saturation, distinct compressed placements condition the mapping of the remaining atoms. Seed orderings and a single-edge cut sweep expose alternative growth trajectories. Reduction shares completed fragments, and a separate decoder combines reusable binary-symmetry event responses within a requested count window. On 1,851 Golden reactions, the saved decoded minimum-plus-five window confirms reference inclusion for 1,832 reactions with one seed and 1,841 with ten seeds, with three and two unresolved reactions, respectively. On 47,974 EnzymeMap reactions, it confirms 47,796 references (99.629\%), with conclusive reaction-level accounting. Mean compute costs for matching plus reduction and for decoding are 1.31 and 1.88 CPU seconds per one-seed Golden reaction, and 4.41 and 8.75 seconds per EnzymeMap reaction. These measure inclusion among retained alternatives, not top-ranked prediction. A separately paired Golden study finds 99.35\% catalogue coverage for MAPPA and 96.92\% for an expanded SLAP sweep; default SLAP is faster, while MAPPA search is 10.38 times cheaper than the expanded sweep on their common completed cohort. The decoder's fixed ordinary-boundary model, bounded starting-map improvement and selected symmetry orbits define explicit limits on event interpretation and coverage.
