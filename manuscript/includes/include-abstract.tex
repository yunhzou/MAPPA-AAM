Atom mapping assigns corresponding atoms across a chemical reaction, but the same endpoints can support several assignments, and symmetry can multiply their explicit representations. We introduce MAPPA, a graph-based method that retains alternative fragment placements in compressed form. Fragment growth asks only whether one compatible assignment exists. When growth saturates, a separate operation constructs the distinct compressed placements, which condition the subsequent mapping of the remaining atoms. A cut sweep varies the growth conditions to explore alternative fragment boundaries. After final family aggregation, a separate decoder collects unique fragments, shares local bond-event calculations and composes them under the saved joint constraints. A completed validation on all \CurrentGoldenTotal{} Golden reactions recovers the annotated correspondence among the retained alternatives for \CurrentGoldenRecovered{} reactions (\CurrentGoldenPercent\%), with all matching, decoding and verification tasks completed and no unresolved verification outcomes. Recovery measures reference inclusion rather than top-ranked prediction accuracy. MAPPA provides an inspectable representation of atom correspondence and bond changes for subsequent chemical assessment.
